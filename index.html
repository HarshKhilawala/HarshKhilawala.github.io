Introduction

Since 2022, my academic and research journey has been driven by a deep curiosity about the human brain — its computational elegance, its adaptability, and its mysteries. During my under undergraduate studies, I got an opportunity to attend the NeuroPSI lab, CNRS - French National Centre for Scientific Research to work on a neuroinformatics project focused on simulating the spiking behavior of biological neuronal network models which at first I got too frustrated to it but with time, I discovered the immense potential of computational methods to unravel the principles of neural function. This experience marked the beginning of my passion for computational neuroscience, where biology and technology converge to help us understand the very foundation of cognition and consciousness.

One of my ultimate career goals is to contribute to the global effort to understand and combat Alzheimer’s disease. (© 2024 Springer Nature Limited. All rights reserved.) This in itself is like to live with a curse. Even the smallest contribution toward alleviating this suffering would, to me, represent a meaningful step toward serving humanity through science.

Research Project - PyNN  

As part of my personal research project to extend my contributions to PyNN, I am still working on a solution to answer the issue of a Neuro-informatics simulator’s compatibility with other widely adopted simulators out there. I have been working on PyNN (https://neuralensemble.org/PyNN/) Package starting summer of 2024 which is aimed to eliminate the need of its user to have knowledge in programming which obstructs widespread adaptation among scientific and academic community. PyNN was developed with a purpose to eradicate the same dependency issue which earlier required to learn simulating neuronal network models towards mainly 4 Major simulators i.e. NEURON, NEST, Brian 2 and Arbor. Simulators as all the 4 simulators mentioned above require their users to have proficiency to write computer programs with their programming paradigm.

Towards the above challenge, I planned out my approach to develop PyNN (GUI) with compatibility support to be able to allow users to run any model developed with majority simulators (+ compatibility of PyNN to run models which are listed on ModelDB - a Database to Support Computational Neuroscience Models).

The above solution would benefit the scientific community my many folds. How?

  1. Widespread adaptation among scientific and academic communities allowing MILLIONS of users from all across different backgrounds to be able to run and simulate neuronal network models which would open new doors of opportunities for next generation. This would be achieved by GUI as it eradicates the need of learning programming which would result in allowing Millions of users (including academic institutions) to simulate neuronal network models without any hassle to learn programming.
  2. Unlocking new capabilities to allow users to simulate the models ON-THE-GO on their mobile devices anywhere through Cloud Integration.
  3. Would open doors of opportunities to develop innovative commercial products by major companies and agile startups.

All the above can be achieved at a minimal cost without any need of high infrastructure and its (minimal) maintenance.
